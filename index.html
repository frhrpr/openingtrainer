How it works

You play opening moves against a simulated opponent. The opponent's moves are picked randomly, weighted by how often real players at the selected Rating play each move in the Lichess database. So a 1200-rated opponent will play the moves that 1200s actually play (including bad ones), while a 2200 opponent will stick to theory longer.

After each of your moves you get feedback: how popular it was, win/draw/loss rates, and what alternatives exist. When the current position has fewer games than the Min games threshold, the game stops and Stockfish gives a verdict on the exit position. If your last move took you out of book, Stockfish also evaluates that specific move so you can see whether the departure was sound.

Settings

Rating
Which Lichess rating bracket to pull stats from. Affects both the opponent's move choices and the popularity/win-rate numbers shown in feedback. Lichess ratings run ~200 points above chess.com at the same skill level.
Speeds
Which time controls to include (bullet, blitz, rapid, classical). Different time controls produce different opening choices — bullet games see more offbeat lines. You can select multiple.
Min games
How many games must exist in the database for a position before the trainer declares you "out of book" and ends the game. Lower values let you explore deeper sidelines; higher values keep you in well-established theory. Default is 20.

Navigation

Click any move in the move list to jump to that position and see its feedback. Use ← → arrow keys or the nav buttons to step through. Home / End jump to start/end. Click the board to return to the live position and continue playing.

Feedback labels

While you're in book, moves are classified by popularity at your rating (book rank and share of games). When you leave book, Stockfish evaluates that one move and classifies by centipawn loss. After the game ends, labels appear in the move list: ✓ = best/good, ~ = inaccuracy, ? = mistake.

Best / Good (in book)
Top book move, or a frequently-played second/third choice.
Best / Good (departure)
Engine cpLoss ≤ 30. (cpLoss = how much the position's evaluation dropped versus the engine's preferred move.)
Inaccuracy
A rare-but-played sideline, or engine cpLoss between 30 and 80.
Mistake
A move not in the database at all (and not engine-best), or engine cpLoss above 80.

Stockfish runs locally in your browser via WebAssembly. Only the departure move and the final position are evaluated, so the engine has plenty of time per call; in-book moves are classified from database statistics alone. The Lichess opening explorer (with a small offline fallback) provides the book data.

Settings are saved in your browser (localStorage) and persist across sessions. Requires internet access for the Lichess API.
